# Audit and direction, Nov 14 2026

A full review of throwindice before the next build push. Three parts: what a new visitor experiences (usability), what the code carries (ponytail critique), and where the project should go. The ordered work that follows from it is in `HANDOFF.md`.

## The one-paragraph verdict

throwindice is two products in one page: a calm **index-fund atlas** ("Investing, simply": regions, sectors, commodities, fund cards that adapt to where you live) and **Live Ball Bounce**, an arcade runner where the candle chart *is* the level and you swipe long, short, cash or all in. The atlas is useful but not unique; plenty of sites list ETFs. **The game is the unique part.** Stock-market "games" on the stores are brokerage screens without real money (buy/sell forms, live tickers), and "Duolingo for finance" apps are quizzes with streaks. Nobody else makes market ideas (trend, bull and bear, cash as a position, compounding, leverage risk) something you *feel* in a 90-second arcade run. So the direction is: **the game is the front door, the atlas is the depth behind it.** Play it, then see the real funds it was about.

The look, speed and polish are already at a level most indie projects never reach (glass 3D art, 60 fps, 0.85 MB first visit, clean accessibility). What stops it being usable is not polish. **It isn't live, the game's balance is broken (standing still out-earns playing well), and nothing brings a player back tomorrow.**

## 1. Usability: a new visitor on a phone

Walked through as a first-time visitor (390 × 844, light and dark): landing, home, menu, fund browsing, fund details, Basics, Token World, a first game run and the results card. Ranked by how much each one costs a real visitor:

| # | Finding | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| U1 | **Not live.** GitHub Pages isn't enabled, so there are no visitors at all. | Owner: Settings → Pages → Source: GitHub Actions. |
| U2 | **Standing still wins the game** (harness: passive 290/min vs market-reader 144). The core promise, "reading the market pays", is false right now. | B8 re-tune (HANDOFF 1). |
| U3 | **The first thing a visitor sees is a blocking "Where are you?" list of 13 regions**, before any value. The region is genuinely useful (an EU visitor gets UCITS funds like CSPX), but it shouldn't be a gate. | Guess it from `Intl` locale/time zone (Sweden → Europe), show content at once, add a small "Funds for Europe · change" chip. Only ask if the guess fails. |
| U4 | **The game is buried**: one small card under the hero. It is the most distinctive thing on the site. | Make the hero itself the way in ("Play the market" on the 3D scene), plus a `#play` deep link for sharing. |
| U5 | **No reason to come back tomorrow.** Missions and a daily gift exist, but there's no shared daily goal or streak. Runners that last (Subway Surfers) are built on daily challenges and fresh content; finance-learning apps that retain do it with streaks. | **Daily chart**: one seeded run per day, the same for everyone, with a streak and a personal best. Cheap: a seed is the day key. |
| U6 | **Nothing to share.** The site has a share card, but runs don't. | A results-card "Share" that renders a 1200 × 630 image of the run (the chart you rode, score, best bank) and calls `navigator.share`. Combined with U5 this is the growth loop: today's chart, your score, a friend tries to beat it. |
| U7 | **"Full details" jumps to a panel further down the page**, under another section, so the visitor loses their place. | Open details as a sheet over the cards, with Back. |
| U8 | **Jargon and repeats.** "Pick a pile", "Pick a planet" and "Swings" are cute but unclear; "VOO vs a savings account" appears on Home and Basics. | Plain labels ("Pick a country", "Price swings"); keep the comparison in one place. |
| U9 | **The bridge from game to atlas is missing.** The game teaches bull/bear, cash, leverage and patterns, but never points to the real-world idea afterwards. | The results card's lesson line links to the matching Basics card or fund pile ("Banking at the top = taking profit → how index funds avoid timing"). |

Already good: contrast and labels pass in both themes; no console errors anywhere; one finger works everywhere; reduced motion is respected; first visit is fast.

## 2. Code: ponytail critique

The repo is lean where it matters: one file, no framework, no build, no package.json, zero runtime dependencies. Its weight comes from **layering**: a whole older UI kept alive under the new one.

```
delete: the pre-Apple design layer. 143 `body.apple` overrides and 155 `!important` win over base rules that are never seen. Fold the overrides into the base and delete what they override. About -35 to -50 KB of 105 KB of CSS. [index.html <style>]
delete: the hidden non-Apple UI: bottom nav, token coin, invest popup, coin bubble, their spin preloaders, the vignette script, and 16 spin sheets. navGame() uses .navbtn[data-page=game] only as a handle; call the opener directly. About -10 KB of HTML/JS, -1.8 MB of assets. [index.html, assets/*-spin.webp]
yagni: GLASS_STYLE=0, the opaque-plastic branches. Glass has shipped. About -30 lines. [scripts/icons/objects.py]
delete: generated files in git: __pycache__/*.pyc (ignored but tracked), dolla_tex*.png (rebuilt by dolla_texture.py), scripts/balance/last-run.json. [scripts/]
shrink: docs/QA.md is a 691-line append-only log. Archive the dated history, keep the live checklist. About -600 lines. [docs/QA.md]
shrink: finished task tables in MARKET.md / BALANCE.md / ANIMATION.md, one line each. About -250 lines. [docs/]
yagni: .gitignore is the Vite template in a no-build repo. -15 lines. [.gitignore]
```

`net: about -900 lines of docs/config, -45 to -60 KB of index.html (of 438 KB), -1.8 MB of assets; -0 deps possible.`

What should stay: the single file (a build step would add more than it saves), the inline fund data (34 KB, the product's content), the harness and the Blender generators (they are what makes the art reproducible).

## 3. Where the project goes

**What it is good for, honestly:**
- **A teaching tool and a portfolio piece.** A beginner can feel what long, short, cash and leverage do in a minute, then see real index funds explained plainly, in their own region. That is rare and it's the strongest use today.
- **Not a broker or an advice product.** Keep it that way. "Not advice", no real tickers in the game, dated and sourced facts in the atlas. Broker affiliate links would undermine that trust and bring financial-promotion rules into play; don't add them early.

**How it becomes usable, in order:**
1. **Go live** (U1). Nothing else matters until people can open it.
2. **Make the game honest** (U2, B8): reading the market must beat standing still.
3. **Remove the first-visit gate and open the front door** (U3, U4).
4. **Give it a daily reason and a share** (U5, U6): the daily chart with a streak and a share card is the smallest loop that can grow on its own.
5. **Bridge game to atlas** (U9, U7, U8).
6. **Cut the dead layer** (section 2) so the next year of work happens in half the CSS.
7. **Then measure**: the run log already records each player's runs on their own device; if the owner wants numbers across players, add a privacy-friendly counter (no cookies) only after launch.
8. **Only then consider an app-store wrapper.** It's already an installable web app (manifest, icons). A native wrapper is worth it only if day-7 return looks good.

**Conclusion:** the craft is done. The look, the speed and the system are past the point where more polish moves anything. What's left is product work: ship it, make the game's core promise true, and add the one daily loop. After the queue in `HANDOFF.md`, throwindice is a finished, shareable thing: a beginner opens a link, plays today's chart in a minute, understands why holding through a bear hurts and why cash is a position, and taps through to the plain-language page about the real funds.
