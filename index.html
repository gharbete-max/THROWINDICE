BASICS

Basics

Short and simple.

Investing in plain words

  • 🏦

    A fund is like a savings account you do not touch. You put money in, you leave it for a long time, and you do not need to read the news every day.

  • 🧺

    ETF means exchange traded fund. It is a basket of many companies that you buy and sell like one share. If one company falls, the others help carry it.

  • 😌

    Passive means the fund just follows a list. It does not pay experts to pick winners, so it costs less and asks less of you. For decades, studies such as the S&P SPIVA reports have found that most actively managed funds trail their index over long periods. SPIVA ↗

  • 🇺🇸

    VOO is the benchmark many people use. It follows the S&P 500, the 500 biggest US companies, such as Alphabet (Google) and Microsoft. When those companies grow, VOO grows. When they fall, it falls.

  • 📈
  • 🌍

    Pick your flavour. You can choose other countries or sectors you like. Do not overthink it: a global fund works great too if you do not want to spend time on it.

  • ⚠️

    Be careful with hype. People may push single stocks, crypto or the latest trend. One company can fall a lot, even to zero. A fund spreads the risk over many companies, so one bad one hurts less.

Investing can fall as well as grow. Learn first, start small. Educational only, not financial advice.

🎯 Why not pick single stocks?

  • 🎲

    Picking winners is very hard. A well-known study of US stocks from 1926 to 2016 found that only about 4% of them created all the extra gains over Treasury bills. Most stocks did worse than Treasury bills. Bessembinder 2018 ↗

  • 🧑‍💼

    Experts fail too. Full-time fund managers with teams and data mostly trail a plain index fund over long periods. SPIVA ↗

  • 💥

    One stock can go to zero. A broad fund holds hundreds or thousands of companies, so no single company can wipe it out.

  • ⏳

    Time helps a broad fund. Historically, the longer people held a broad fund, the narrower the range of results. It is never risk-free and the future can differ.

  • 🎮

    You can still pick stocks. Many people enjoy it. If you do, keep it to a small amount you can afford to lose, and keep the rest in funds.

BASICS

What is tokenization?

Short and simple.

  • 🪙

    A token is a digital record on a blockchain. It says who owns what, like a receipt that anyone can check.

  • 🧱

    Tokenization means putting a real thing on that record. A fund share, a government bill, gold or a dollar can each be represented by a token. A token is only as good as the promise behind it: who holds the real asset, and can you cash it in?

  • ⚡

    Why people try it. Backers talk about faster settlement, trading around the clock and smaller pieces. These are goals, not guarantees.

  • 🌱

    We are early. Few products exist, many are only for professional investors, and rules differ by country.

  • ⚠️

    The risks are different from a fund. The issuer can fail, the code can have bugs, and you can lose your wallet keys. Rules and protections may be weaker than for a normal ETF.

  • 🔎

    Always ask what you really own. Check the legal claim, who holds the real asset, and whether you can redeem it.

Educational only, not financial advice.

🔭 What to watch (4 quick points)
What is worth watching?Not just token prices: watch the asset behind the token, the network it uses, the legal rights it represents, and the institutions connecting the two worlds.
ASSET BACKINGWhat does it represent?

Fund units, government debt, private credit, property or a claim on something else.

NETWORKWhere is it recorded?

Public or permissioned chain, validators, uptime, congestion and interoperability.

RIGHTS & RULESWhat can holders claim?

Redemption terms, transfer restrictions, legal ownership and investor eligibility.

ADOPTIONIs it actually being used?

Outstanding value, active wallets, transaction activity and repeat institutional use.

WALLETS

Digital wallets

Where your tokens live, and how to keep them safe.

  • 🔑

    A wallet holds keys, not coins. Your tokens stay on the blockchain. The wallet keeps the secret key that proves they are yours.

  • 🏦

    Two kinds of custody. With a custodial wallet, an exchange or broker keeps the keys for you: easier, but you trust them. With self-custody you keep the keys: full control and full responsibility.

  • 🔥

    Hot or cold. A hot wallet is online, like a phone app: handy but easier to attack. A cold wallet is an offline device: safer for larger amounts.

  • 📝

    The seed phrase is everything. 12 or 24 words that can rebuild your wallet. Anyone who has them can take everything. Never share them or type them into a website. Nobody can reset them for you.

  • 📮

    An address is like an account number. You can share it. Check every character before you send, because transfers usually cannot be undone.

  • ⛽

    Sending costs a network fee. It goes to the network, not to the app, and it changes with how busy the network is.

  • 🎣

    Watch out for scams. Fake support, fake giveaways and urgent messages are common. Real support never asks for your seed phrase.

  • 🧺

    ETFs need no wallet. Crypto and blockchain ETFs sit in a normal broker account, so the broker handles the keys.

🏦 Brokers

Brokers & intermediaries

See what each participant does in the chain — without buy/sell buttons or broker rankings.

One asset can involve several firms. A broker may provide client access, while a custodian safeguards assets, an issuer creates the token, and a transfer agent or platform maintains ownership records. These roles can overlap, but they are not interchangeable.
01 / CLIENT ACCESS

Broker or investment platform

Connects a client to eligible products, provides account interfaces and may handle orders or reporting. Token access depends on jurisdiction, product and the platform's permissions.

Useful to inspectEligibility · fees · disclosures · transfer options
Does the platform support this specific tokenized product, or only conventional funds? Can the asset be transferred out? Who is the legal counterparty? What happens if the platform stops operating?
02 / SAFEGUARDING

Custodian

Safeguards underlying assets or private keys, depending on the structure. Some arrangements use regulated custodians; others rely on self-custody or specialist providers.

Useful to inspectKey control · segregation · recovery process
Who controls the keys? Are client assets segregated? Is there independent assurance? How are lost keys, insolvency and unauthorized transfers handled?
03 / PRODUCT & RECORDS

Issuer or transfer agent

Defines the product and rights, creates or authorizes tokens, and may maintain the official ownership register or reconcile on-chain records with legal records.

Useful to inspectProspectus · redemption · register · legal venue
What exactly does the token represent? Which record is legally authoritative? Who can freeze, burn or reissue tokens? What are the redemption terms?
04 / TECHNICAL RAILS

Network & settlement provider

Provides blockchain infrastructure, transaction validation, wallet connectivity, token standards or links between traditional payment systems and on-chain records.

Useful to inspectUptime · network fees · interoperability · resilience
Is the network public or permissioned? Can assets move to another network? Who pays transaction fees? What is the recovery plan during an outage or bridge failure?
